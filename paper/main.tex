\begin{abstract}
Attributions produced by explainable AI (XAI) methods are often read as
evidence of what drives a financial forecast, yet they are rarely checked
against a known answer. In this paper, a planted-signal recovery benchmark is
constructed on real market data. Six synthetic features with known relations
to the target (a linear signal at two strengths, a sign-coded nonlinear
signal, pure noise, and an exact and a partial copy of a real feature) are
injected into a panel of \nTickers{} large-cap stocks, and
\nXaiMethodsCatalogued{} XAI methods are catalogued across \nModelsZoo{}
model architectures. Recovery, defined as ranking a planted signal above the
noise feature on held-out data, is treated as a necessary condition for a
faithful explanation and not as a sufficient one. All \nXaiNativeScored{}
methods that yield a feature-importance vector recovered the two strong
signals on every model tested. At the level of individual stocks, TreeSHAP,
KernelSHAP, and LIME recovered all three signals in every stock, whereas
permutation importance recovered the strong linear signal in
\pctSymPermutation\% of stocks and did not recover the weak one. The credit
assigned to duplicated features differed widely between methods, and two
concept-based scores could not be distinguished from those of random
directions. The pipeline, configuration, and results are publicly released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Explainable AI (XAI) methods are increasingly used to justify financial
models to regulators, risk committees, and end users. An explanation is
called \emph{faithful} when it reflects the features on which the model
actually relies. Faithfulness cannot be taken for granted. Popular post hoc
methods often disagree with one another~\cite{krishna2024disagreement};
several gradient-based methods were found to be no better than a random
ranking when image classifiers were retrained after feature
removal~\cite{hooker2019roar}; and some saliency methods were shown to be
insensitive to the randomization of model parameters and of training
labels~\cite{adebayo2018sanity}. When such methods are applied to financial
forecasters, the quality of the resulting attributions is therefore unknown.

A direct remedy is to evaluate explanations against a known answer. Such
benchmarks exist for synthetic tabular data, graphs, images, and
text~\cite{liu2021xaibench,agarwal2022openxai,agarwal2023graphxai,lee2025xaiunits,li2023m4},
as reviewed in Section~\ref{sec:related}. To the best of the authors'
knowledge, none of them plants known signals inside a real financial
forecasting panel, where market noise, non-stationarity, and cross-sectional
dependence are present.

In this paper, such a benchmark is constructed. Six synthetic features are
injected into a real panel of \nTickers{} US large-cap stocks in which, as is
verified, the real features carry almost no out-of-sample predictive signal.
Each XAI method is then scored on whether the planted signals are ranked
above a planted noise feature on held-out data.

The scope of this test is stated explicitly. The relation between each
planted feature and the target is known by construction, but the extent to
which a fitted model relies on the feature is not. The benchmark therefore
measures \emph{signal recovery}, which is a necessary condition for
faithfulness: a method that fails to recover a learnable signal cannot be
considered faithful, whereas a method that recovers it has not thereby been
shown to be faithful.

The contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item A planted-signal recovery benchmark is constructed on a real financial
panel, with a linear signal at two strengths, a sign-coded nonlinear signal,
a noise feature, and an exact and a partial copy of a real feature, all
scored out of sample.
\item \nXaiMethodsCatalogued{} XAI methods are catalogued across
\nModelsZoo{} models. The \nXaiNativeScored{} methods that yield a
feature-importance vector are scored on their own output, and the remaining
\nXaiNotScored{} are reported as not scored.
\item Four widely used attribution methods are compared stock by stock, and
the scope of the accompanying inference is delimited.
\item The credit that each method assigns to duplicated features is
reported without a target value being assumed.
\item Null checks are applied to concept-based and mechanistic outputs,
which are reported as exploratory.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Attribution methods.} SHAP is an additive feature-attribution
framework based on Shapley values; its model-agnostic estimator, KernelSHAP,
is obtained by weighted linear regression, with feature independence among
the simplifying assumptions of its derivation~\cite{lundberg2017shap}.
TreeSHAP is its variant for tree ensembles~\cite{lundberg2020treeshap}. LIME
fits an interpretable model locally around a
prediction~\cite{ribeiro2016lime}. Permutation importance measures the loss
in performance after the values of one feature are shuffled, a procedure
introduced for random forests~\cite{breiman2001random}. Integrated Gradients
attribute the prediction of a deep network to its inputs on an axiomatic
basis~\cite{sundararajan2017ig}.

\textbf{Benchmarks with known answers.} XAI-Bench releases synthetic data
sets with known distributions, from which ground-truth Shapley values and
other metrics are computed~\cite{liu2021xaibench}. XAI-Units pairs synthetic
data sets with models of known behaviour, in the manner of unit
tests~\cite{lee2025xaiunits}. GraphXAI provides a synthetic graph generator
with ground-truth explanations together with real-world graph data
sets~\cite{agarwal2023graphxai}. OpenXAI combines a synthetic generator with
seven real-world tabular data sets, including credit data, but its
ground-truth metrics apply only to synthetic data or to logistic-regression
models~\cite{agarwal2022openxai}. $\mathcal{M}^4$ evaluates attribution
methods on real image and text data, mainly with faithfulness metrics that
require no ground truth~\cite{li2023m4}.

\textbf{XAI in finance.} A recent survey provides a taxonomy of XAI
approaches for financial time-series
forecasting~\cite{arsenault2025xaifinance}; it is a literature review and
reports no check of explanations against a known answer. The present study
is positioned between the two lines of work: known signals are planted, as
in the synthetic benchmarks, but inside a real financial panel.

\section{Test Bed and Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:testbed}
\subsection{Data and Features}
The FNSPID data set~\cite{dong2024fnspid} is used; it provides daily prices
per ticker and time-aligned financial news. The universe consists of \nTickers{}
US large-cap stocks, selected from \nCandidates{} sector-diverse candidates
by data availability alone (at least \minNewsDays{} news days and a complete
price history for \dateMin--\dateMax). The panel contains \nObs{} stock-day
rows from \panelStart{} to \panelEnd; news is present on \newsCov\% of them.

For each stock and trading day $t$, \nFeatPrice{} price-based features (four
lagged returns, two rolling volatilities, a 14-day RSI, a 20-day
moving-average gap, the high--low range, the open-to-close return, log
volume, and a 20-day volume $z$-score) and \nFeatSent{} news-sentiment
features (FinBERT~\cite{araci2019finbert} and
Loughran--McDonald~\cite{loughran2011liability} scores, news counts, and
short rolling aggregates) are computed from information available at the
close of day $t$. News published after the close is assigned to the next
session. The targets are the next-day log return $r_{t+1}$ and its direction
$d_{t+1}=\mathbf{1}[r_{t+1}>0]$. Daily returns with $|r|>\returnClip$ are
treated as data errors and masked; this rule is fixed in the configuration
and is applied before any split.

\subsection{Models and Walk-Forward Protocol}
The model zoo comprises \nModelsZoo{} models: logistic regression, a
decision tree, a random forest, XGBoost~\cite{chen2016xgboost}, LightGBM,
CatBoost, a support vector machine, $k$-nearest neighbours, an explainable
boosting machine (EBM)~\cite{nori2019interpretml}, a multilayer perceptron
(MLP), a GRU, an LSTM, a 1D convolutional network (CNN), a temporal
convolutional network (TCN), a small Transformer, and a graph neural network
(GNN) over a cross-stock correlation graph. The last \lookback{} trading
days are supplied to the sequence models.

An expanding-window walk-forward protocol with \nFolds{} test years is used
(Table~\ref{tab:folds}). For test year $Y$, the training set contains all
rows dated more than \embargoDays{} calendar days before 1~January of $Y$;
such an embargo between training and test rows is recommended when
observations are not independent over time~\cite{lopez2018advances}.
Features are standardized with statistics of the training rows only. No
hyperparameter search is performed and no validation split is used for model
selection: every hyperparameter is fixed in advance in the released code,
and the neural sequence and graph models are trained for a fixed number of
epochs. The only internal hold-outs are the early-stopping splits that the
library implementations of the MLP and of the EBM draw from their training
rows.

\subsection{Substrate Check}
The real features carry almost no out-of-sample signal. On the direction
task, the mean accuracy of the \nModelsZoo{} models over the \nFolds{} folds
is \modelZooMeanAccPct\%, against \majAcc\% for the majority-class baseline,
and the largest absolute Matthews correlation coefficient is
\modelZooMaxMCC. Adding the sentiment features to the price features changes
directional accuracy by between $\sentDeltaMin$ and $\sentDeltaMax$
percentage points for the \nSentModels{} models with stored per-stock
predictions; the stock-resampled 95\% interval contains zero for XGBoost and
LightGBM and lies below zero for logistic regression
($[\sentCIloLogistic,\,\sentCIhiLogistic]$). The planted features described
next are therefore the only signals in the panel whose relation to the
target is known.

\section{Planted Features and Scoring}
\label{sec:planted}
\subsection{Planted Features}
Six synthetic features are added to the panel (Table~\ref{tab:planted}).
They are generated once with a fixed seed and are all present at the same
time. For scoring, models are trained on inputs augmented with these
features (\nFeatTotal{} features in total), separately from the models of
Section~\ref{sec:testbed}; the accuracy of the augmented models is raised by
the planted signals and is not a measure of forecasting skill.

The correlations of the two linear signals are the designed values; those of
the sign-coded signal and of the partial copy are the realized values. The
sign-coded signal depends on the next-day return only through its sign, so
it is a nonlinear function of the return; its realized correlation with the
return itself is \gtNlCorrRet. In the released data, the names of the six
columns carry the prefix \texttt{gt\_}.

\subsection{What Recovery Measures}
\label{sec:meaning}
The planted features fix the relation between a feature and the target, not
the function that a fitted model learns from it. Three consequences follow.
First, no ``true importance'' value is assigned to any planted feature; only
its ordering against the noise feature is scored. Second, the sign-coded
signal is the nonlinear effect of a single feature. No interaction between
distinct inputs is planted, and no claim about the recovery of interactions
is made. Third, no target value is asserted for the credit given to a
duplicated feature. A fitted model is free to rely on either of two
identical columns, so the share of credit that would be correct depends on
the model and is not known. Credit shares are therefore reported
descriptively.

\subsection{Scoring}
\label{sec:scoring}
\textbf{Catalogue level.} Each method is applied to every model for which it
is defined; a method is never forced onto an architecture that it does not
support. The model is trained on all rows before \scoreYear{}
(Table~\ref{tab:folds}, last row) and the global importance vector of the
method is computed on the rows of \scoreYear. No embargo is applied at this
stage, so the target of the last training day is realized on the first test
day. A planted signal counts as recovered on a model if its importance
exceeds that of the noise feature.

\textbf{Stock level.} For four widely used methods, the analysis is repeated
stock by stock on a single XGBoost classifier (400 trees of depth~4,
learning rate 0.03, row and column subsampling of 0.8). For every stock, an
importance vector is computed from the rows of that stock in \scoreYear:
for TreeSHAP, the mean absolute SHAP value over 52 randomly drawn rows; for
KernelSHAP, the same quantity over 40 rows, with 50 background rows and 150
perturbation samples per row; for LIME, the mean absolute weight over 10
rows; and for permutation importance, the mean drop in accuracy over 10
shuffles of all rows of the stock. The \emph{margin} of a planted signal is
its importance minus that of the noise feature. The four importances are
expressed in different units (log-odds for TreeSHAP, probability for
KernelSHAP, weights of a local surrogate for LIME, and accuracy for
permutation importance). A margin is therefore compared only with zero and
never between methods. Methods are compared through a unit-free quantity,
the share of stocks in which the margin is positive. For the two copies, the
\emph{credit share} $a/(a+b)$ is reported, where $a$ and $b$ are the
importances of the copy and of its source.

\subsection{Inference and Its Scope}
\label{sec:inference}
Intervals are percentile bootstrap intervals from \nBootstrap{} resamples of
whole stocks. The model is fitted once and every importance vector is
computed once; neither is repeated within the bootstrap. The intervals
therefore describe the variation of the mean margin over the stocks of this
universe, conditional on one fitted model and one run of each explainer.
Three sources of uncertainty are not covered. First, variation due to the
training seed is not measured. Second, variation due to the sampling of the
explainers themselves is not contained in the intervals; a partial
indication is given in Section~\ref{sec:stability}. Third, the stocks are
not independent: the mean pairwise correlation of their daily returns is
\faithRhoBar, which under equal correlation corresponds to about \faithEffN{}
independent units, and serial dependence within the test year is not
modelled. Resampling stocks leaves this dependence in place. The intervals
and the paired tests below are therefore reported as descriptive summaries
and not as exact significance statements.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Recovery Across the Catalogue}
Table~\ref{tab:catalogue} lists the \nXaiMethodsCatalogued{} methods.
\nXaiNativeScored{} of them yield a feature-importance vector and are scored
on that output. The remaining \nXaiNotScored{} are not scored on feature
recovery. Anchors, counterfactual search, and the contrastive explanation
method return rules or examples for individual predictions. The three
Grad-CAM variants return a curve over time steps and not over features; a
per-feature score obtained with Integrated Gradients on the same CNN is
stored with the released results, but it is a result of Integrated Gradients
and is counted once, under that method. The concept-based and mechanistic
tools are treated in Section~\ref{sec:exploratory}.

SHAP, LIME, permutation importance, Integrated Gradients, LRP, guided
backpropagation, deconvnet, Grad-CAM, the EBM, and GNNExplainer are taken
from reference libraries. Anchors, counterfactual search, the contrastive
explanation method, RISE, Grad-CAM++, and Score-CAM are simplified
re-implementations adapted to tabular or one-dimensional inputs; in
particular, RISE was proposed for image classifiers~\cite{petsiuk2018rise}
and is applied here by masking features at random. The rank-aggregated
TreeSHAP row averages the within-instance ranks of local TreeSHAP values,
and the surrogate row is a shallow decision tree fitted to the predictions
of the XGBoost model.

All \nXaiRecoveredLinear{} scored methods ranked the linear signal above the
noise feature, and all \nXaiRecoveredNl{} did so for the sign-coded signal,
on every model on which they were scored. At this signal strength and at the
level of a global importance vector, recovery therefore does not
discriminate between methods. The comparison is sharpened below by lowering
the signal strength and by scoring each stock separately.

\subsection{Stock-Level Recovery}
Table~\ref{tab:core} reports the stock-level results. TreeSHAP, KernelSHAP,
and LIME ranked each of the three planted signals above the noise feature in
every stock, including the weak signal ($\rho=\gtRhoWeak$), and the
intervals of their margins lie above zero. Permutation importance behaved
differently. The linear signal was ranked above the noise feature in
\pctSymPermutation\% of stocks (interval
\pctSymCiLoPermutation--\pctSymCiHiPermutation\%), the sign-coded signal in
\pctSymNlPermutation\%, and the weak signal in \pctSymWeakPermutation\%,
with a weak-signal margin whose interval contains zero. The weak signal was
thus recovered by \nWeakRecovered{} of the \nCoreMethods{} methods.

In the paired comparison on the linear signal, permutation importance failed
in \mcnemarPermOnlyWrong{} stocks in which each of the other three methods
succeeded, and the reverse occurred in \mcnemarOtherOnlyWrong{} stocks
(exact McNemar test, $\mcnemarPExpr$ for each pair, subject to the caveats
of Section~\ref{sec:inference}). A likely contributor, which was not tested,
is that the per-stock permutation importance is estimated from changes in
accuracy on one year of daily rows of a single stock, which is a coarse
statistic for a signal of this strength.

The margins of the linear and the sign-coded signal are not compared with
each other, because the two signals are not matched in strength with respect
to the direction label: the realized correlation of the sign-coded signal
with the label is \gtNlCorrDir, whereas the corresponding value for the
linear signal was not stored.

\subsection{Credit Assigned to Duplicated Features}
For the exact copy, the mean credit share was \creditExactTreeshap{} for
TreeSHAP, \creditExactKernelshap{} for KernelSHAP, and \creditExactLime{}
for LIME; for the partial copy, the shares were \creditPartialTreeshap,
\creditPartialKernelshap, and \creditPartialLime. The two SHAP estimators
thus assigned about a fifth of the credit of the pair to an exact copy and
nearly half to a partial copy, whereas LIME assigned a little over a third
in both cases. The ordering of the methods is not the same in the two cases.
For permutation importance, the share is defined for only
\nCreditPermutation{} of the \faithUniverseN{} stocks, because stocks in
which the two importances sum to a non-positive value are excluded; its
values are therefore not comparable with the others. No share is taken to be
the correct one. As noted in Section~\ref{sec:meaning}, part of this pattern
may be a property of the fitted model and not of the explainer, since an
even split is expected only if the model itself relies on both columns
equally.

\subsection{Stability}
\label{sec:stability}
Three checks that are stored with the results bear on the stability of these
findings.

\textbf{Scored year.} The stock-level analysis was repeated with each of the
years \yearRobustFirst--\yearRobustLast{} as the scored year. In these
repetitions the model is trained on all other years, so for the years before
\scoreYear{} the training set includes later years; the repetitions are not
out-of-time evaluations and test only whether the pattern depends on the
scored year. TreeSHAP, KernelSHAP, and LIME ranked the linear signal above
the noise feature in every stock in each year, and permutation importance
did so in \yearPctSymMinPermutation--\yearPctSymMaxPermutation\% of stocks.
The credit share of the exact copy ranged over
\yearCopyCreditMinTreeshap--\yearCopyCreditMaxTreeshap{} for TreeSHAP,
\yearCopyCreditMinKernelshap--\yearCopyCreditMaxKernelshap{} for KernelSHAP,
and \yearCopyCreditMinLime--\yearCopyCreditMaxLime{} for LIME, whereas that
of permutation importance changed sign ($\yearCopyCreditMinPermutation$ to
$\yearCopyCreditMaxPermutation$).

\textbf{Choice of stocks.} When \jackNDrop{} of the \faithUniverseN{} stocks
were left out at random (\jackNIter{} draws), the mean margin of each method
changed by at most \jackMarginDeltaMax\%.

\textbf{Explainer sampling.} In the robustness stage, \scoreYear{} was
scored a second time with a different random selection of explained rows.
Relative to Table~\ref{tab:core}, the margin of the linear signal changed by
\rerunDeltaTreeshap\% for TreeSHAP, \rerunDeltaKernelshap\% for KernelSHAP,
and \rerunDeltaLime\% for LIME. These differences are small, but they are
not contained in the reported intervals.

\subsection{Agreement Between Rankings}
For pairs of methods that were run on a common model, the Spearman rank
correlation between the full importance rankings was averaged over the
shared models (\agrCommonMin{} to \agrCommonMax{} models per pair). TreeSHAP,
KernelSHAP, and LIME agreed with one another (\agrKernelshapTreeshap{} for
TreeSHAP and KernelSHAP, \agrLimeTreeshap{} for TreeSHAP and LIME, and
\agrKernelshapLime{} for KernelSHAP and LIME), whereas the rankings of
permutation importance were only weakly related to theirs
(\agrPermMin--\agrPermMax). Methods that share no model with these four are
not included.

\subsection{Concept-Based and Mechanistic Outputs}
\label{sec:exploratory}
The following outputs are not recovery scores and are reported as
exploratory.

\textbf{TCAV.} TCAV quantifies the sensitivity of a prediction to a concept
that is represented as a direction in the activation space of a
layer~\cite{kim2018tcav}. For two concepts defined on the GRU (days with
high news volume and days with positive sentiment), the raw TCAV score was
\tcavScoreMax{} in both cases. A statistical test of TCAV scores is
prescribed by the original method~\cite{kim2018tcav}. As a null check, each
score was compared here with the scores of \tcavNRandom{} concept vectors
fitted to random relabelings of the same activations. The random vectors
produced mean scores of \tcavNullMeanRange{} with a wide spread, and neither
concept was distinguished from them (empirical two-sided $p=\tcavPEmp$). The
raw scores are therefore not evidence of concept sensitivity.

\textbf{Sparse autoencoder.} A sparse autoencoder of the kind used for
language models~\cite{cunningham2024sae} was trained on the last-token
representation of the Transformer. For \saeObserved{} of the \saeLatents{}
latents, the absolute correlation with at least one input feature exceeded
\saeThreshold. Under \saeNShuffles{} shuffles of the feature columns the
maximum count was \saeNullMax, and across \nSensRuns{} training runs
(\sensNSeeds{} seeds, \sensNYears{} test years) the share of such latents
was \sensPctInterpMin--\sensPctInterpMax\%. The latents thus track input
features. This finding is correlational: it does not show how the
representation is computed, and no claim about the linearity of the
mechanism is made.

\textbf{Component ablation.} Each of the eight attention heads and the two
encoder layers of the Transformer was ablated in turn. This is a manual
sweep and not the automated circuit-discovery algorithm
of~\cite{conmy2023acdc}. In each of the \nSensRuns{} runs, exactly one of the
\ablNComponents{} components exceeded the threshold of $z>\ablThreshold$,
and in every run it was a full encoder layer
(\texttt{\sensMajorityComponent} in \sensMajorityN{} of the \sensTotalRuns{}
runs); no single head exceeded the threshold. An earlier single run with the
configuration of one of these runs found no component above the threshold,
which illustrates the variation between runs. Because the ablation of a full
layer removes all of its heads, a larger effect is expected for a layer than
for a head, and under a shuffle null about one component (\ablNullMean{} on
average) exceeds the threshold by chance. These observations are therefore
not interpreted as evidence of a circuit.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
\textbf{Recovery is not faithfulness.} The benchmark establishes whether a
method surfaces a signal that is present in the data and learnable. It does
not establish how a model uses that signal. A direct test of faithfulness
requires models whose dependence on each input is known by construction, as
in unit-test benchmarks~\cite{lee2025xaiunits}; such controlled models were
not built in this study.

\textbf{Planted constructions.} Every planted signal is the effect of a
single feature. No interaction between distinct inputs is planted, the
strengths of the linear and the sign-coded signal are not matched, and no
target is defined for the credit shares under collinearity.

\textbf{Inference.} The intervals condition on one fitted model and one run
of each explainer. Variation between training seeds was not measured, and
dependence between stocks and over time is not modelled
(Section~\ref{sec:inference}).

\textbf{Methods not scored.} The Grad-CAM variants were not scored, since no
signal that is localized in time was planted, and the rule-based and
example-based methods were not scored on recovery. Several methods are
simplified re-implementations.

\textbf{Exploratory analyses.} No direct test of the representation, such as
a linear probe, and no structure-aware null for the ablation were carried
out.

\textbf{Scope.} One panel (\nTickers{} US large caps, daily data,
\dateMin--\dateMax) and one scoring task (direction) were used, and at the
catalogue level one scored year and one fit per model. Other markets,
horizons, or constructions may change which methods recover the signals.

\section{Conclusion}
A planted-signal recovery benchmark was constructed on a real financial
panel, and \nXaiMethodsCatalogued{} XAI methods were catalogued across
\nModelsZoo{} models. All \nXaiNativeScored{} methods that yield a
feature-importance vector recovered the two strong planted signals on
held-out data, so recovery at this strength did not separate them.
Differences appeared at a finer resolution. Stock by stock, TreeSHAP,
KernelSHAP, and LIME recovered every signal in every stock, whereas
permutation importance did not recover the weak signal. The credit assigned
to duplicated features depended strongly on the method, and raw TCAV scores
were not distinguishable from those of random directions. Recovery is a
necessary condition for faithfulness and not a proof of it. Controlled
models with known dependencies, planted interactions and time-localized
signals, and repeated training runs are identified as the next steps.
